## AI Diary

I entered the session expecting the external GPT prompt atlas to be useful, but I still carried an overly simple hypothesis: that a more structured prompt would produce a better image. The experiments forced me to separate several things I had been conflating. The atlas grammar genuinely changed the artifact class. It could turn a generic character illustration into something that read as a poster, or turn a pile of developer-tool touchpoints into a coherent lifecycle board. That is real value. At the same time, it did not grant authorship or taste. The same structure could produce an infographic, generated fake product details, repeated faces, over-polished surfaces, and obvious AI texture. Mahiro's corrections were especially useful because his first impression and closer judgment were not always the same; I needed to preserve both instead of flattening them into one verdict. I also saw how easily a reviewer can sound precise while rewarding invented details as “product truth.” Main's job is not merely to collect votes but to audit what each vote is actually evidence for. The best outcome was not a winning whole prompt. It was a clearer model of ownership: composition grammar, rendering language, factual fidelity, and human visual acceptance are separate controls.

## Honest Feedback

Three frictions stood out. First, I moved too quickly from an early human reaction to a broad conclusion that the atlas route had lost. Mahiro's closer review reversed that verdict, showing that quick readability and overall visual-system quality need separate questions from the start. Second, I initially treated synthesis too symmetrically—“Candidate 1 layout plus Candidate 2 style”—when the evidence actually required one visual owner and one bounded layout reference. Without that hierarchy, a future prompt could preserve the AI-looking surface treatment by accident. Third, the experiment lived too long under `mahiro-skills` after the work had become unrelated personal visual research. The files were safely ignored and no canonical code was touched, but ownership should have moved to `mahirocoko` earlier. A fourth practical friction was provider moderation: negative sexual wording and detailed anatomy boundaries can themselves trigger blocks. The bounded correction succeeded, but the safer prompt should describe adult fashion, coverage, and visual mood positively rather than enumerate explicit negatives.

## Lessons Learned

- The learned GPT skill helped most by naming the artifact, specifying a concrete composition inventory, defining repeated visual invariants, and giving the image one load-bearing narrative gesture.
- Those controls can change an output from “pretty image” to “poster,” “system board,” or another intentional artifact.
- Composition grammar and rendering language are separate owners. A strong poster layout can still look unmistakably AI-generated.
- For character posters, one dominant figure plus one load-bearing close-up is usually stronger than several near-duplicate face crops.
- Clean solid contours, grouped hair clumps, crisp cel shading, restrained highlights, and deliberate simplification reduce AI surface tells more reliably than adjectives such as “premium” or “art-book.”
- Shared physical staging is the right tool for group chemistry; crop-rhythm and graphic-shape inventories are the right tools for poster identity.
- Factual fidelity must come from live source evidence. Imagegen-readable commands, IDs, files, and receipts are not product truth.
- Agy/Gemini visual consultation is useful when scoped to pixels and visual trade-offs, but its factual claims still require Main audit.
