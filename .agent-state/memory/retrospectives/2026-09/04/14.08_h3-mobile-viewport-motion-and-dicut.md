## AI Diary

I felt the session become useful only when I stopped treating each provider report as the truth and kept returning to the pixels. The Gemini 3.7 result was the clearest warning: its metric said the green excess was zero, yet the actual hair, coat, and boot edges still looked olive. I had also routed to an outdated Gemini version even though Mahiro expected the current 3.8 model. Both errors were correctable, but they exposed the same weakness—relying on a plausible procedural story instead of checking the exact current owner and rendered artifact. Gemini 3.8 produced a materially better extraction, but even then I found stale hashes in its report and had to separate media correctness from report correctness. The idle pass taught me another version of that lesson. I initially reused the transition's center crop because both provider outputs were 1152×640; direct inspection showed that the portrait panel had shifted right, making the supposedly deterministic crop half black. I am glad I caught that before dicut, but the better behavior is to treat every generated transport envelope as untrusted until its actual content bounds are measured. The cleanup was also important emotionally: instead of leaving a maze of “proof” folders that future me might misread as equally valid, I preserved one current accepted lineage, one pending candidate, and compact historical failure owners.

## Honest Feedback

Three frictions mattered. First, I used Gemini 3.7 when Mahiro's active expectation was 3.8. The fix was not merely changing the next command; I had to preserve the rejected evidence and update durable routing memory so the mistake would not recur. Second, my first dicut review relied too much on the producer's chosen metric. A verifier or report can be internally consistent while measuring the wrong visible failure, so Main must inspect actual composites and define a metric from the observed artifact rather than accepting a convenient number. Third, I assumed one XIMI pillarbox crop would generalize across jobs. That shortcut created a half-black idle crop even though the source ratio and provider container looked similar. Content bounds, alignment, and crop ownership need per-result evidence. A fourth friction was process overhead around the report-hash correction: the callback job latched onto an old terminal state, and my first monitor used zsh's read-only `status` variable. Neither affected media, but both added avoidable recovery work. The direct, receipt-bound follow-up and corrected monitor were the right recovery.

## Lessons Learned

- XIMI's `video-minimax` generic image array can produce useful ordered motion, but it does not prove hard first/last endpoint semantics.
- A 1152×640 provider envelope does not establish a reusable portrait crop. Detect the actual content panel for every result.
- Intentional viewport crop and provider-introduced reframe are different contracts; only Mahiro can accept the latter as the new visible baseline.
- `g <= max(r, b)` is not a sufficient green-spill gate for dark or warm edges because visibly olive pixels can satisfy it.
- Semantic matte, chroma-hole rejection, physical background unmixing, and hue-aware despill worked for these red/black/gold sources, but rendered review remains required.
- Final media hashes must be computed after all writes and re-read before a terminal report; delivery text is not proof.
- Rejected raster evidence should become a compact historical owner once its transferable failure lesson, task identity, prompt, hashes, and successor are preserved.
- Cleanup must update active manifests and research at the same time so deleted paths do not remain executable context claims.
