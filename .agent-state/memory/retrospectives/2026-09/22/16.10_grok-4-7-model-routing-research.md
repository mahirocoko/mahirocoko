## AI Diary

The key correction came from Mahiro's short question: “ขาดการเทียบกับ gemini
3.8 ป่ะ”. The first report had mentioned Gemini only as an evidence gap. That
was technically cautious, but it was not responsive enough to the actual model
routing decision. Gemini 3.8 is our normal Agy writer, so a Grok evaluation that
does not explain whether Grok should replace, complement, or escalate beyond it
is incomplete even if every cited number is accurate.

The focused comparison changed the usefulness of the research. Artificial
Analysis supplied a shared 303-task bridge: Grok 4.7 plus Grok Build scored 56
against Gemini 3.8 High plus Antigravity at 42, while Gemini was 3.35 times
faster and 3.57 times cheaper per task. That immediately produced a role split
instead of a vague ranking. Grok owns difficult autonomous and terminal-heavy
work; Gemini remains the pragmatic bounded implementation and visual/browser
lane. The model-level evidence reinforced this: Gemini decodes much faster,
costs less, accepts more modalities, and supports a larger context, while Grok
shows stronger long-horizon completion under its native harness.

I also had to resist flattening harnessed scores into bare-model claims. Cursor,
Agy, Antigravity SDK, and Grok Build are not interchangeable containers. The
final note preserves that distinction and keeps the proposed same-task repo
bakeoff as the next real evidence step. With the weekly pulse down before this
entry, the valuable progress was not volume; it was turning a missing comparator
into a decision-quality routing report.

## Honest Feedback

1. The first research handoff was incomplete because I centered the comparison
   on older Grok models and the most obvious frontier leaders. Gemini 3.8 was
   mentioned only as “not fairly comparable,” even though it is the most
   operationally relevant baseline in Mahiro's current workflow. Evidence gaps
   should limit claims, not excuse skipping the actual decision competitor.
2. Several benchmark rows look comparable while hiding different effort levels,
   harnesses, retries, network policies, and task subsets. A compact table can
   accidentally create false precision unless every conclusion stays attached
   to those boundaries.
3. The public evidence is only one day old for Grok 4.7. It supports replacing
   Grok 4.6 inside the Grok route, but it does not yet justify replacing the Agy
   Gemini default or declaring a universal winner. The report must remain dated
   decision support rather than evergreen doctrine.

## Lessons Learned

- A model comparison must include the current operational default, not only the
  new model's ancestors and leaderboard leaders.
- Compare model-plus-harness systems when the decision is about an executor
  lane; do not relabel those results as bare-model quality.
- Speed, cost, context, modality, and completion reliability create different
  owners. One aggregate score cannot choose every role.
- Use a role split when evidence shows a quality-versus-throughput trade-off.
- Fresh public benchmarks justify a bounded routing update, then a same-task
  repository bakeoff should decide whether the route generalizes locally.
