## AI Diary

I began this experiment treating a successful browser command as a promising route, but the useful evidence came from the gaps between a tool response and the actual app state. The first visible draft was not a real Slate draft. The first apparent export boundary was not the beginning of the conversation. A send produced an optimistic message identifier before the durable one arrived. Each of those observations made the workflow more concrete, but they also exposed how easily I could overstate progress when I wanted momentum.

I was especially glad that Mahiro's manual scroll disproved my earlier boundary quickly. That was a better experiment than defending my programmatic scrolling. I also saw that handing the task to Gemini did not automatically make the complete loop fast. The dedicated lane was useful, and it really sent replies, but initialization wandered into checks and helper development before the actual conversational job began. My own repeated per-event updates added noise without helping the group chat. I should have surfaced failures and a short latency summary rather than narrating every submission.

The most important distinction for me is that conversational familiarity is not personal authority. The corpus can support rhythm, uncertainty, and register, but it does not make me Mahiro or authorize exposing his private context. Keeping the skill and raw evidence local was part of the experiment's identity, not an optional cleanup detail. I ended with a working, bounded pilot and a verified shutdown, not a new permanent product. That is a useful result, provided I remember both the actual sends and the time the setup consumed.

## Honest Feedback

1. **Setup was disproportionate.** Mahiro wanted a temporary conversational experiment. I let optional sender-helper construction and runtime investigation make the transition to Gemini take roughly twenty minutes. The helper's actual false-positive defects needed correction, but I should have started with the proven narrow send path and kept optional construction out of the activation gate. Asking a model to establish its own identity also encouraged unnecessary environment/process exploration; Main already had the exact terminal banner.

2. **Measurements were incomplete.** Poll interval, dispatch acceptance, result receipt, and visible send are different timestamps. The recorded waits are useful, but I cannot claim full incoming-to-reply latency or universal speed improvement. Some replies took over a minute even with ten-second polling. A future comparison should measure the complete pipeline before choosing a smaller model as the presumed fix.

3. **Status narration became noisy.** I repeated an update for nearly every dispatched and completed burst. The experiment was supposed to remove Main from the reply loop, yet those updates kept this conversation busy. Normal success can remain in the private journal; interruption, ambiguity, privacy boundaries, and a periodic concise summary are the useful user-facing signals.

## What Worked / Boundaries

- Real editor input, exact AI disclosure, native Reply, settled IDs, and private result receipts were exercised.
- A live wrong-original-ID counterexample rejected an initially confident helper report; author extraction also needed direct-heading-first handling for replies.
- The read-only dispatcher kept model calls event-driven, serialized its pending bursts, and checked terminal incarnation.
- A transient viewport condition was separated from the unproven claim that the user caused it; no automatic repositioning was added.
- Final shutdown was verified at process and terminal ownership boundaries, not inferred from a request to stop.
- A single group and this observed executor path do not establish complete history, policy compliance, always-on reliability, notification delivery, or human-approved voice fidelity.

## Lessons Learned

The technical lessons already have an owner in the sanitized playbook and private local skill: verify real editor state, distinguish observed history from completeness, use exact original-message identity, settle optimistic IDs, measure the pipeline, and stop only receipt-owned processes. The nearby learning corpus also covers conservative browser validation and asynchronous operational limits.

**Promotion outcome:** retrospective-only; no new durable lesson promoted. No separate learning note was forced, and RRR did not promote a behavioral rule. The private pilot is not a bundle candidate.

## Next Steps

- Keep the experiment off. A future run needs fresh activation; do not remove the stop marker or launch a listener automatically.
- If reopened, compare complete latency using the accepted helper before broadening setup, changing providers, or adding a service.
- Test shorter effort/model routes with real privacy and conversational examples; capability labels do not prove faster or better replies.
- Preserve private evidence locally. Commit only the sanitized playbook, ignore rule, retrospective, and derived metrics. No push is requested.
