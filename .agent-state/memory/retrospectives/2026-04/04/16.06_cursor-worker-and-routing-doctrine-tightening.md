## AI Diary

This session felt like one of those days where the abstract picture finally became operational. I spent a lot of time in a week with +122.2% messaging velocity turning worker policy into something I can actually execute without improvising every decision. What I liked most was that the Cursor integration did not require theatrics. Once I accepted that it should mirror the Gemini boundary only where that mirror was useful, the whole design stayed thin and legible. The docs, tests, and runtime checks all lined up in a way that made the system feel dependable rather than just clever.

The doctrine work was more subtle, but it mattered just as much. I could feel that the repo had enough pieces now — Gemini, Cursor, MCP, caching, routing, model choices — that vague principles were starting to become a liability. Without a practical routing table and explicit escalation triggers, I would still have to re-derive policy on every task. That’s expensive in attention, not just in tokens. I’m glad I pushed the document from broad philosophy into procedure without bloating it into a giant spec. That balance is hard: too little and future behavior drifts, too much and nobody can actually use the file. Right now it feels close to the right size.

## Honest Feedback

Three friction points stood out. First, I still needed several rounds of external confirmation before I trusted the Cursor model naming and policy enough to encode it. That was the right cautious move, but it shows how easy it is to confuse product-facing names with real runtime identifiers. Second, the doctrine editing had a tendency to sprawl. Every good addition created pressure for another one, and I had to keep resisting the urge to turn `AGENTS.md` into a full operating manual. Third, there is still some repetition in the final document around verification and authority boundaries. It is not harmful, but it is a sign that this topic has been added incrementally rather than composed from a single rewrite. It works now, but future edits should keep an eye on density.

## Lessons Learned

- Runtime model identifiers should be verified from the actual CLI before they are baked into policy.
- Worker doctrine becomes useful only when it bridges philosophy to procedure.
- A good orchestrator document needs enough repetition to protect invariants, but not so much that it turns into policy fog.
