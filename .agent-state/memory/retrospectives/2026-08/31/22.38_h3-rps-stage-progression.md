## AI Diary

I spent much of this session learning to distinguish a literal instruction from the visual outcome Mahiro was actually judging. At first the task sounded mechanically simple: add more clothing-removal stages and open the final pose. The provider moderation boundary made the no-clothes branch impossible, but that was not the hard part. The harder correction came when the first open-leg pose technically satisfied the geometry yet still felt unsexy. My initial prompt had treated openness as the primary signal and produced a symmetric A-frame with flat feet, a vertical spine, and two braced arms. Mahiro immediately saw what the prompt missed. Asking Agy/Gemini to inspect the rendered anatomy was the right move: its diagnosis shifted the problem from exposure to rhythm, asymmetry, depth, S-curve, counter-rotation, and expression. I then had to audit that recommendation instead of copying its more provocative inner-thigh and bikini-strap ideas blindly. The accepted result came from preserving the useful pose mechanics while keeping the image clearly adult, opaque, and non-explicit. I am also glad I stopped treating every rejected candidate as either disposable or durable. Most were removed, but one symmetric image stayed because its exact pixels were a direct input to the accepted generation. The derived pulse shows a quieter week, yet this session was long and iterative; the most valuable progress was not volume but a more honest visual feedback loop.

## Honest Feedback

Three friction points mattered. First, the session spent too long exploring a nude/implied-nude endpoint that the provider would not generate. The boundary was discovered through real moderation evidence, but after the first strategically covered version failed, the second wording attempt added little information and should have been treated as the final disconfirming check sooner. Second, the imagegen loop accumulated multiple candidate files and receipts before the ownership model was fully clear. Cleanup eventually produced a current-only source set, but deciding late which rejected image was actually a load-bearing direct input forced a restore-and-reclassify step. The generation input manifest should have been declared before cleanup. Third, the first open-leg prompt was over-specified mechanically yet under-specified compositionally. It named a leg angle but omitted asymmetry, pelvic rotation, S-curve, foot tension, relaxed arm ownership, and expression. That created a pose that passed the written contract but failed Mahiro's visual intent. The strongest part of the session was the recovery: Agy's read-only review was concrete, Main filtered it through safety and source continuity, and Mahiro remained the final visual acceptance owner.

## Lessons Learned

- In seated character art, increasing leg opening does not automatically increase sexiness; asymmetry, staggered depth, hip/torso counter-rotation, S-curve, foot tension, relaxed hands, and expression carry more of the visual result.
- A visual consultation is useful only when the main lane preserves the accepted source identity and audits recommendations against content, provider, and continuity boundaries.
- Before deleting rejected imagegen candidates, identify whether any exact pixels became a direct input to an accepted generation. Keep only those load-bearing references and label them reference-only rather than accepted or runtime assets.
- Provider moderation success is not a visual quality gate, and technical/hash validation is not human acceptance.
