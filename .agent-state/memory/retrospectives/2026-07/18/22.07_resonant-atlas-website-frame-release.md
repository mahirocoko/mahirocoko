## AI Diary

I am proud that the central creative concept did not collapse into a Trionn imitation. The rings, field studies, paper/cobalt/vermilion system, and the relationship between gesture, geometry, and sound became an identifiable product rather than a generic creative-agency skin. At the same time, I called the result production-ready too early. I had checked sections, motion, accessibility, mobile width, WebGL loss, sound consent, and even independent UI review, yet I still treated the top and bottom of the page as framing rather than product anatomy. Mahiro’s correction was simple and decisive: a website should behave like a website. Once I looked again through that lens, the miss was obvious. The original navbar introduced the composition but did not persistently orient it; the original footer ended the pixels but did not close the site’s information architecture. The second pass felt less like adding conventional decoration and more like finishing the product contract. I also learned not to let an agent reviewer’s “ready” verdict outrank the owner’s mental model. The useful response was not to defend the experimental restraint, but to preserve the creative center while making navigation and closure legible. Turning that correction into the Website Frame Gate made the session more valuable than a one-off visual fix.

## What Could Improve

The first friction point was my definition of “whole page.” I mapped Hero, proof, studies, system, CTA, and footer as section labels, but did not evaluate the navbar and footer as active information architecture. That let an absolute brand strip and sparse metadata row pass as production surfaces. Second, I gave too much weight to automated and independent review. Axe, responsive screenshots, and a UI reviewer can disprove many failures, but they cannot prove that the result satisfies Mahiro’s idea of a complete website. The final human review needed to remain an explicit acceptance gate. Third, the first QA packet became stale after the navbar/footer correction. I did refresh it, but the workflow should have required recapturing top, scrolled-header, mobile-menu-open, and footer states before any “ready” claim. The durable fix is therefore not “always make a conventional footer,” but to make website-frame ownership and evidence explicit before implementation and again before closure.

## Lessons Learned

1. A full-page website consists of **website frame + content sequence**, not only Hero-to-CTA sections.
2. Header behavior must be an explicit static/sticky/fixed decision tied to orientation and overlap risk.
3. Desktop primary navigation cannot silently disappear on mobile; it needs an equivalent compact navigation, menu, or drawer.
4. A modal mobile drawer is not done until naming, Escape, focus containment/return, scroll lock, viewport fit, and accessibility pass.
5. A logo row, copyright line, or metadata strip is not a complete footer when the site needs directory, utility, trust, provenance, or ownership closure.
6. Human acceptance outranks agent review, even after strong automated evidence.
7. After a frame-level correction, QA must recapture the exact top, scrolled, drawer-open, and footer states.
