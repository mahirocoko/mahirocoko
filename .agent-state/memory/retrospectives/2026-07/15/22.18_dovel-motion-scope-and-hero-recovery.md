## AI Diary

I felt confident during the first motion passes because every local check was passing, but the session repeatedly reminded me that technical completeness is not the same as matching Mahiro's intended scope. My first notable mistake was treating “we can do this without GSAP” as a design principle. CSS could have handled basic reveals, but Mahiro was asking for controlled choreography and smooth sequencing. Once challenged, I corrected quickly and the resulting hybrid system was materially better. The larger mistake came later: I heard “Hero overall still does not pass” and combined that sentence with my own prior concern about the triptych composition. I then did good work on the wrong problem—auditing product proof, extracting cutouts, and recomposing the Hero—while Mahiro only meant the Hero motion. The work was careful, but the interpretation was not. I am glad I stopped after the clarification, exposed the decision explicitly, and reverted every unapproved artifact rather than defending the new direction. On day four of the current streak, the most valuable learning was not another animation technique; it was that design nouns like “overall,” “feel,” and “not complete” inherit context from the immediately active lane. I need to preserve that conversational scope before escalating from motion polish to composition reset.

## Honest Feedback

1. **I overreached from ambiguous feedback.** The active conversation was about motion, so “Hero overall” should first have been interpreted as the complete Hero motion sequence. A one-line scope confirmation would have prevented a long, unnecessary composition and asset lane.
2. **I initially made tool choice ideological.** Saying GSAP was unnecessary before defining the choreography contract was backwards. Smoothness does not come from a library alone, but timeline ownership, responsive sequencing, interruption handling, and cleanup made GSAP justified here.
3. **Review labels became too optimistic.** Several scoped reviewers returned PASS while Mahiro still saw incomplete coverage. The reviews were valid for their prompts, but my summaries sometimes sounded broader than the evidence. Every verdict needs an explicit surface and state scope.

There was also some avoidable review churn: one agent timed out, another reported reduced-motion clipping that exact DOM geometry disproved. Independent review is useful, but it cannot replace direct measurement. The best recovery was to reproduce the finding, measure bounds, and ask for a geometry-based recheck instead of changing correct CSS.

## Lessons Learned

- Keep the current conversational lane as the default meaning of ambiguous feedback. During a motion pass, “overall” means the overall motion unless the user explicitly reopens composition.
- Ask one bounded scope question before changing anatomy, assets, IA, or product proof from feedback that could also mean timing/coverage.
- Choose motion tools after defining ownership: shared-object continuity, section entrances, surface-specific mechanics, and local state transitions are different jobs.
- One universal ScrollTrigger timeline creates off-screen completion. Local groups should trigger from the surface users can actually see.
- Motion completeness includes initial, mid, settled, fast-scroll, back-scroll, missing-image, reduced-motion, and cleanup states.
- Browser-special elements such as `legend` can ignore intuitive parent padding behavior. Rendered geometry should determine spacing ownership.
- A strong wrong-scope implementation should still be reverted when the owner clarifies intent; quality does not retroactively authorize scope.
