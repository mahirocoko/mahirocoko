## Key Decisions

- Do not treat payment-gateway support as evidence of authorized game inventory.
- Do not approve a supplier merely because it has an API, consumer catalog, cashback, or Thai company registration.
- The recommended MVP hypothesis is Thailand-only, THB, Dynamic PromptPay, one or two games, no customer wallet, no marketplace, one supplier route per SKU, manual review, and daily reconciliation.
- A research handoff must make ownership, evidence standards, RFI questions, unit economics, stop gates, and final decision outcomes explicit; a long narrative alone is not enough.
- Borrow process grammar from Fable Method, not its full skill surface. Mahiro Code already has DoD, fresh verification, and subagent orchestration; the real gaps were intent gating, retry stopping, and claim verdicts.
- Keep the new workflow in memory-only dogfood first. Promote it into `mahiro-skills` standard/template/tests only after at least three medium/high-risk tasks show real value without excess ceremony.

## What Could Improve

- The initial game-top-up research answer underweighted Thai local dealer systems such as WePay. The first supplier map should have included an explicit local-agent/dealer lane from the start.
- The first handoff draft conflated closing research with being ready for production. Future handoffs should define allowed outcomes and the boundary between research and implementation before expanding sections.
- The first unit-economics template risked counting FX spread twice and treated gateway reserve too close to a contribution-margin cost. Finance formulas need an independent model check before being called reusable.
- The Fable deep-learn outputs were comprehensive but large. A parent synthesis pass remains necessary so future use begins from the hub and key deltas rather than reading more than 3,000 lines by default.
- The conversation accumulated several adjacent research phases. Earlier checkpoints could have made the transition from market research to process learning more explicit.

## Honest Feedback

Three friction points deserve to remain visible. First, public evidence creates a false sense of progress. A provider can have a polished API page, active app, DBD registration, and attractive catalog while still lacking downstream resale rights, reliable refunds, or safe pre-funded balance terms. The research had to keep saying “not publicly verified,” which feels repetitive but is the honest boundary. Second, deep documentation can become another form of completion theater. The packet became genuinely transferable only after adding owners, decision outcomes, reusable registers, and the exact questions another person should ask; line count was never the quality gate. Third, workflow borrowing is easy to overdo. Fable Method has memorable branding and a clean think/act/prove story, but Mahiro Code already had overlapping structures. Installing or copying it wholesale would likely create duplicate triggers and ceremonial overhead. The right adaptation was the smallest missing behavior, followed by dogfood. Future external-method studies should begin by mapping overlap and delta before proposing a new skill or global rule.

## Lessons Learned

- Separate `payment accepted` from `game value delivered`; every platform decision becomes clearer after that split.
- Supplier authorization is a mandatory gate, not a weighted score that cheap pricing can offset.
- Research handoffs need owners, allowed decisions, evidence hierarchy, reusable registers, and stop conditions.
- Treat `redeemable_code` as one delivery taxonomy unless PIN/code behavior actually differs.
- Use all-in executable FX rates or reference rate plus spread, never both.
- External workflow learning should identify the missing delta before importing a product surface.
- Conditional intent gates and fresh claim-verification judges are useful only on ambiguous or medium/high-risk work.
- A technical `VERIFIED` verdict remains separate from Mahiro's product acceptance.
